Objective

Identify which customer segments are most likely to churn and quantify the revenue at risk, so retention efforts can be targeted where they matter most.

Churn Overview & Contract Impact

The overall churn rate is 27.9%. Contract type is the single strongest predictor: month-to-month customers churn at 40.8% versus 14.9% on two-year contracts — a 2.7x difference.

Tenure & Price Sensitivity

New customers (0–6 months) churn at 45.4% — onboarding is the danger zone. Churn also rises steadily with monthly charges, pointing to price sensitivity in the top billing tier.

Payment & Service Patterns

Electronic-check payers churn more than autopay customers, and fiber-optic subscribers churn more than DSL — likely a mix of price and unmet speed expectations.

Key Findings

  • Month-to-month contracts drive the majority of churn (41% rate).
  • The first 6 months are critical: early-tenure churn is 1.6x the average.
  • Higher monthly charges correlate with higher churn — value perception is slipping at the top end.
  • An estimated $0.67M in annual revenue is lost to churn.

Recommendations

1. Contract migration campaign: offer month-to-month customers a discounted annual plan; even a 10-point conversion would cut churn materially.
2. Fix onboarding: proactive check-ins at 30/60/90 days for new customers, when churn risk peaks.
3. Win back autopay: incentivize electronic-check users to switch to bank transfer or card.

Methodology

Exploratory analysis in Python (pandas): churn rates segmented by contract, tenure, charges, payment method, and service type. Visualizations with matplotlib. Dataset: synthetic telecom customer records generated for portfolio demonstration.
